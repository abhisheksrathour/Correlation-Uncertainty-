Layerwise thickness tolerances do not determine joint coating errors. We test whether reducing first-order covariance risk necessarily improves nonlinear manufacturing reliability in broadband antireflection coatings. Six design strategies are compared for eight dispersive SiO₂/HfO₂ layers over 450–750 nm and 0–40°, using 4,096 independent evaluation draws per error process. At 4 nm RMS thickness error, correlation-envelope (CE) optimization raises independent-error specification yield from 67.53% to 74.98%, compared with 76.20% for learned covariance. Under common-mode errors, CE yields 81.93% but has 20.06% 95th-percentile maximum reflection, versus 13.16% for nominal and 6.37% for sampled CVaR. Nonlinear versus first-order quantile discrepancies are design- and process-dependent (nominal IID: 8.35% versus 6.06%). A six-start audit (512 draws per design) reveals marked basin dependence: the median IID yield is zero for every method, although selected solutions perform substantially better. An exploratory 2-to-4 nm discrepancy indicator shows pooled magnitude association (Spearman ρ = 0.668, n = 18), with inconsistent with in-process trends. An additional 1,024-realization Hessian test across all 48 design–process combinations improve per-realization maximum-reflectance prediction in 47/48 cases and 95th-percentile prediction in 44/48, but retains important exceptions.
